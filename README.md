#  __Regulation or Pipelines? An Experiment on Permian Flaring__
##  Project description
Gas flaring in the Permian Basin has fallen sharply even as oil production hit records, breaking the historical proportionality between the two. This project asks why that decoupling happened - specifically, whether New Mexico's 2021 **[venting-and-flaring](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2022-06/NewMexico-state-profile.pdf)** rule actually changed operator behavior, or whether new pipeline takeaway capacity and gas economics would have driven the same decline without it. Rather than use flaring to predict production (a relationship the decoupling itself renders unstable), it treats the structural break as the object of study, estimating when and how sharply the flaring-to-production elasticity shifted. Identification comes from a within-basin natural experiment: the Delaware sub-basin straddles the New Mexico - Texas line, so New Mexico's dated regulatory "treatment" can be compared against the Texas side, which had no equivalent rule, in a difference-in-differences design. The signal is VIIRS satellite-detected flaring - independent of what operators report to their own regulators - with pipeline in-service dates serving as a falsification test (they should move both states) and state-reported flaring volumes as a cross-check against the sensor's detection-floor artifact. The contribution is a data-driven, regulator-independent verdict on whether the Permian's flaring decline reflects genuine regulatory compliance or merely infrastructure catching up.
## Problem Definition
Permian flaring has fallen sharply while oil production hit record highs over the past decade. We ask whether New Mexico's 2021 **[venting-and-flaring](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2022-06/NewMexico-state-profile.pdf)** rule actually changed operator behavior, or whether pipeline takeaway capacity and gas economics would have produced the same decline regardless. The Delaware sub-basin straddles the NM-TX line. They have the same geology, often the same operators, but only New Mexico imposed a dated treatment (98% gas-capture rule, capture requirements effective April 1, 2022). This supports an experiment with Texas as control. The primary signal being VIIRS satellite-detected flaring. The study is independent of operator self-reports. The pipeline in-service dates provide a falsification test, and state-reported volumes detection cross-check. Unit of analysis: county-month within the Delaware sub-basin (Jan 2019-present). We will not predict production from flaring or sanctioning individual operators; and we will not extrapolate outside Delaware.
